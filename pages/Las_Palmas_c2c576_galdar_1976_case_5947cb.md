## Introduction

Gáldar became Spain’s big UFO story because two different kinds of evidence became fused into one dramatic legend. On 22 June 1976, a large luminous display was seen from several Canary Islands and from a Spanish Navy corvette at sea. That part of the case is well attested: Spain’s Air Force opened a 107-page file, later declassified, and the event sits within a documented run of Canary Islands sightings in the 1970s. The more disputed part is the close-encounter claim near Las Rosas, in the municipality of Gáldar, where a doctor said he saw a transparent blue sphere containing two very tall red-clad figures. That vivid witness story made the case famous, but later missile-based reconstructions strongly weakened the idea that anything physical was hovering over the onion fields of north-west [Gran Canaria]({{ 'gran-canaria-1974/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?id=38141" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa+2Naukas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

The case still matters for [Las Palmas]({{ 'what-really-happened-over-las-palmas/' | relative_url }}) because it shows, unusually clearly, how a real sky event can become a local close-encounter narrative. The best-supported finding is not that nothing happened. Something striking was seen. The harder question is whether the Gáldar “sphere and giants” story was an independent close-range event, or a human interpretation of the same distant missile plume seen across the archipelago. The balance of evidence now favours the second reading.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/43047669/Los_gigantes_de_G%C3%A1ldar_y_los_avistamientos_canarios" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia+2Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF) Los gigantes de Gáldar y los avistamientos canarios</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PDF) Los gigantes de Gáldar y los avistamientos canarios</span></span></span>

## What Witnesses Reported Across the Islands

The wider 22 June 1976 sighting was not confined to Gáldar. Contemporary and later accounts describe a bright point rising from the horizon, expanding into a large luminous shape, and producing coloured halos or a semi-spherical glow visible from several islands. The Ministry of Defence catalogue identifies the official file as “Avistamiento de fenómenos extraños en Canarias: 22 de Junio de 1976”, produced by the Air Operational Command’s intelligence section, with illustrations, graphics and plans among its 107 pages.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?id=38141" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

This matters because the “mass sighting” part of the story does not depend on one spectacular witness. Reports came from land and sea, including the Spanish Navy corvette Atrevida, which was operating between Fuerteventura and Gran Canaria. Later summaries of the file and local reporting describe the ship’s crew seeing a white light rise, remain apparently fixed, form a bluish circular halo, and then fragment or fade.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.larazon.es/canarias/dos-gigantes-dentro-esfera-azul-testimonio-que-marco-mayor-caso-ovni-espana_2025091068be9b29c752286abd9d9530.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: larazon.es">[La Razón]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">larazon.es</span><span class="citation-popover-title">La Razón“Dos gigantes dentro de una esfera azul”: el testimonio que</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La Razón“Dos gigantes dentro de una esfera azul”: el testimonio que</span></span></span>

For readers trying to understand why the event became so famous, this is the first key point: the sky display itself was not a tiny anecdote. It had duration, multiple viewing points and official attention. It also occurred in a place where the horizon can make distant high-altitude events look close, especially over the Atlantic at night. In Las Palmas province, Gran Canaria, Fuerteventura and the surrounding sea lanes all offered the kind of wide viewing geometry that can turn a missile or rocket plume into a shared spectacle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://openminds.tv/canary-islands-mass-ufo-sighting-1976/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: openminds.tv">[Openminds.tv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">openminds.tv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">canary islands mass ufo sighting 1976</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">canary islands mass ufo sighting 1976</span></span></span>

The second key point is that the descriptions were not all identical. Some witnesses described a large sphere, others a glow or halo, and others a changing luminous structure. That variety is exactly what one would expect when people at different angles, altitudes and distances watch a high-altitude plume. It is also the kind of variety that UFO literature often compresses into a single, cleaner story after the fact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://naukas.com/2016/06/22/dos-gigantes-rojos-cabalgando-un-misil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: naukas.com">[Naukas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">naukas.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">dos gigantes rojos cabalgando un misil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">dos gigantes rojos cabalgando un misil</span></span></span>

## The Doctor, the Blue Sphere and the “Two Giants” Claim

The most famous Gáldar testimony came from Dr Francisco Julio Padrón León, who was travelling at night to visit a patient near Las Rosas, in the Gáldar area, accompanied by others including a taxi driver. According to the account later quoted from the official file and analysed by Spanish sceptical researchers, the car’s headlights caught or framed a faintly luminous sphere that the doctor interpreted as close to the ground. He described it as transparent, bluish and enormous, with two very tall red-clad figures visible inside.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://naukas.com/2016/06/22/dos-gigantes-rojos-cabalgando-un-misil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: naukas.com">[Naukas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">naukas.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">dos gigantes rojos cabalgando un misil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">dos gigantes rojos cabalgando un misil</span></span></span>

That story gave the case its memorable shape. A mass light display over the Canary Islands might have become one more 1970s UFO file; the image of “two giants” inside a transparent blue sphere made it a legend. It also changed the evidential problem. A distant luminous phenomenon can be compared with missile launches, photographs, timings and similar cases. A close encounter with occupants depends far more heavily on witness interpretation, memory and the reliability of a single narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/43047669/Los_gigantes_de_G%C3%A1ldar_y_los_avistamientos_canarios" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF) Los gigantes de Gáldar y los avistamientos canarios</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PDF) Los gigantes de Gáldar y los avistamientos canarios</span></span></span>

The doctor’s social status is part of why the story travelled so far. A medical witness sounds more credible than an anonymous rumour, and Spanish UFO writing of the period often treated trained or professional witnesses as especially important. But credibility is not the same thing as accuracy. A sincere witness can misjudge distance, scale and location, particularly at night, on rough roads, under stress, and while seeing an unfamiliar luminous phenomenon that is actually far beyond the local landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dokumen.pub/unidentified-flying-objects-briefing-document-the-best-available-evidence-0307492273-9780307492272.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dokumen.pub">[dokumen.pub]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dokumen.pub</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on dokumen.pub.</span></span></span>

The official and sceptical doubts focus on that mismatch. If a 30-metre or larger transparent sphere with two occupants had been hovering low over Las Rosas, it should have produced a very different evidential trail from a distant sky display: stronger local corroboration, physical evidence that survived scrutiny, radar or aviation implications, and consistent close-range observations from nearby witnesses. Instead, the close-encounter element remained far more isolated than the island-wide luminous phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://naukas.com/2016/06/22/dos-gigantes-rojos-cabalgando-un-misil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: naukas.com">[Naukas+2Naukas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">naukas.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">dos gigantes rojos cabalgando un misil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">dos gigantes rojos cabalgando un misil</span></span></span>

## Why the Las Rosas Details Became So Disputed

The dispute is not simply between “believers” and “sceptics”. It is between two ways of reading the same night. One reading treats the doctor’s close-range description as a second event: a nearby craft seen at Gáldar while a broader aerial phenomenon was also visible across the islands. The other reading treats the close encounter as a dramatic misperception of the same distant phenomenon that many others saw from different places.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/43047669/Los_gigantes_de_G%C3%A1ldar_y_los_avistamientos_canarios" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF) Los gigantes de Gáldar y los avistamientos canarios</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PDF) Los gigantes de Gáldar y los avistamientos canarios</span></span></span>

Manuel Borraz’s study, later discussed and republished with commentary by Ricardo Campo, is important because it insists that the Las Rosas incident cannot be assessed in isolation. Borraz’s central point was that the Gáldar claim must be placed against the many other Canary observations made on the same date and at roughly the same time. Once that wider context is restored, the idea of a low-hovering sphere in one field becomes less persuasive than the idea of a distant high-altitude event interpreted locally.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://naukas.com/2016/06/22/dos-gigantes-rojos-cabalgando-un-misil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: naukas.com">[Naukas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">naukas.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">dos gigantes rojos cabalgando un misil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">dos gigantes rojos cabalgando un misil</span></span></span>

The supposed physical trace in the onion field is another weak point. Local recollections and press references speak of flattened or damaged onions near the alleged landing area, but later sceptical commentary treated this as poorly supported and possibly inflated by press attention. A trace case needs careful controls: who first found it, when it was photographed, whether the affected patch had ordinary agricultural causes, whether samples were preserved, and whether investigators ruled out trampling, disease, irrigation effects or local mischief. The Gáldar material does not appear to meet that standard.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://infonortedigital.com/archive/130815/agaete-mi-pasion-cuando-los-extraterrestres-vivian-entre-agaete-y-tenerife-ovni-1976" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: infonortedigital.com">[InfonorteDigital]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">infonortedigital.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Infonorte Digital Agaete mi pasión: cuando los extraterrestres vivían entre</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Infonorte Digital Agaete mi pasión: cuando los extraterrestres vivían entre</span></span></span>

There is also a basic geometry problem. Reports from across the islands point to a large, distant, elevated light display. The doctor’s account relocates something similar into the near foreground. Human perception is especially vulnerable to this kind of error when there are few distance cues. A bright or translucent object seen against the night sky can seem to be just beyond a ridge, road or field when it is actually hundreds of kilometres away and tens or hundreds of kilometres high.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://naukas.com/2016/06/22/dos-gigantes-rojos-cabalgando-un-misil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: naukas.com">[Naukas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">naukas.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">dos gigantes rojos cabalgando un misil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">dos gigantes rojos cabalgando un misil</span></span></span>

## Why the Missile Explanation Changed the Debate

The strongest later explanation is that the 22 June 1976 display was caused by U.S. Navy Poseidon C-3 submarine-launched ballistic missile tests in the Atlantic. This was not a casual guess added decades later. Spanish researchers Vicente-Juan Ballester Olmos and Ricardo Campo argued that several Canary Islands “UFO” events from 1974 to 1979 matched U.S. Navy missile tests in date, timing and visual form. Their work identified the 22 June 1976 event as part of that pattern, rather than as a one-off mystery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/16027101/Identificados_Los_OVNIS_de_Canarias_fueron_misiles_Poseidon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on academia.edu.</span></span></span>

Independent missile chronologies support the crucial date match. A Poseidon C-3 chronology records that USS Von Steuben launched two Poseidon missiles in an operational test on 22 June 1976. Astronautix also lists Poseidon C3 launches on that date at 20:16 and 20:17 GMT, with a high-altitude trajectory. Those timings are close to the evening Canary Islands reports once local time and viewing duration are considered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuclearcompanion.com/data/poseidon-c3-missile-chronology/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuclearcompanion.com">[nuclearcompanion.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuclearcompanion.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">poseidon c3 missile chronology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">poseidon c3 missile chronology</span></span></span>

The visual match is also important. High-altitude missile and rocket plumes seen around twilight can look enormous, silent, slow-changing and strangely coloured. Exhaust gases expand in the upper atmosphere, catch sunlight after local sunset, and form glowing bubbles, fans, spirals or jellyfish-like shapes. To observers on the ground, especially without knowledge of a secret or distant launch, such a plume can look like a structured object rather than a trail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gideonreid.co.uk/giant-aliens-inside-a-huge-crystalline-orb/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gideonreid.co.uk">[gideonreid.co.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gideonreid.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">giant aliens inside a huge crystalline orb</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">giant aliens inside a huge crystalline orb</span></span></span>

## What the Official File Proves — and What It Does Not

The official Spanish file proves that the 22 June 1976 Canary Islands reports were taken seriously enough to be investigated and archived. The Ministry of Defence catalogue confirms the file’s institutional origin, its 107-page length, its subject as UFO observations in the Canary Islands, and its declassification under a July 1994 Air Staff order. That is a solid archival basis for saying the case was officially recorded, not merely invented by later storytellers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?id=38141" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

It does not prove that the doctor saw a landed craft, occupants or a physical object near Gáldar. Official UFO files often preserve testimony without endorsing the witness’s interpretation. In this case, later summaries note that even within the official handling of the case, the occupant story was treated with caution. That distinction is essential: a government file can document a claim without validating the claim’s extraordinary content.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFO_sightings_in_the_Canary_Islands" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO sightings in the Canary Islands</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO sightings in the Canary Islands</span></span></span>

The file also belongs to a broader Spanish declassification story. Spain eventually placed dozens of Air Force UFO files online, and local reporting in the Canary Islands noted that the Ministry of Defence had digitally published 80 declassified reports totalling about 1,900 pages. This made it easier for researchers and the public to compare Gáldar with other Canary incidents instead of treating it as an isolated marvel.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lavozdelanzarote.com/en/news/people/defense-reveals-files-of-ufo-sightings-in-lanzarote_111208_102.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lavozdelanzarote.com">[La Voz de Lanzarote]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lavozdelanzarote.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">La Voz de Lanzarote Defense reveals files of UFO sightings in Lanzarote</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La Voz de Lanzarote Defense reveals files of UFO sightings in Lanzarote</span></span></span>

That comparison weakened the exotic interpretation. The more the Canary cases are read together, the more the repeated pattern stands out: large luminous forms, island-wide visibility, maritime or aviation witnesses, evening timing, and later links to missile activity in the Atlantic. Gáldar remains the most vivid story in Las Palmas UFO history, but it is not the best evidence for a close encounter. It is better understood as the most dramatic witness interpretation attached to a real missile-like sky event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/43047669/Los_gigantes_de_G%C3%A1ldar_y_los_avistamientos_canarios" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF) Los gigantes de Gáldar y los avistamientos canarios</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PDF) Los gigantes de Gáldar y los avistamientos canarios</span></span></span>

## How Later Reporting Strengthened and Weakened the Story

Later reporting strengthened the case in one narrow sense: it kept attention on a real event with official documentation. The 2016 digital availability of Spain’s UFO files and renewed local articles meant that readers could see Gáldar not just as folklore, but as part of a documented Canary Islands archive. Recent Canary press still returns to the story because it sits at the intersection of local memory, [official files]({{ 'official-files-65dacf/' | relative_url }}) and one unforgettable witness claim. La Voz de Lanzarote+2La Razón<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lavozdelanzarote.com/en/news/people/defense-reveals-files-of-ufo-sightings-in-lanzarote_111208_102.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lavozdelanzarote.com">[lavozdelanzarote.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lavozdelanzarote.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">La Voz de Lanzarote Defense reveals files of UFO sightings in Lanzarote</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La Voz de Lanzarote Defense reveals files of UFO sightings in Lanzarote</span></span></span>

But later research weakened the extraordinary version. The [missile explanation]({{ 'missile-theory/' | relative_url }}) accounts for the mass sighting more economically than an unknown craft does, and it fits a run of similar Canary incidents across several years. Ricardo Campo’s rebuttal of the “mass UFO” framing explicitly argues that the 22 June 1976 event was a U.S. Navy Poseidon missile test and that other Canary sightings matched similar tests on 22 November 1974, 19 November 1976, 24 March 1977 and 5 March 1979.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://openminds.tv/canary-islands-mass-ufo-sighting-1976/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: openminds.tv">[Openminds.tv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">openminds.tv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">canary islands mass ufo sighting 1976</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">canary islands mass ufo sighting 1976</span></span></span>

The doctor’s “two giants” account weakened most of all, because it did not gain the kind of independent support needed for a close encounter. It remained memorable, culturally powerful and locally attached to Gáldar, but its evidential status did not rise with time. Instead, as comparative work improved, it looked more like an example of how an extraordinary sky display can be misread through foreground landscape, expectation and the pressure of a frightening or confusing moment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://naukas.com/2016/06/22/dos-gigantes-rojos-cabalgando-un-misil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: naukas.com">[Naukas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">naukas.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">dos gigantes rojos cabalgando un misil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">dos gigantes rojos cabalgando un misil</span></span></span>

## Why Gáldar Still Matters in Las Palmas UFO History

Gáldar remains important because it is a near-perfect case study in how UFO history is made. There was a real stimulus in the sky. There were many witnesses. There was a military investigation. There was a spectacular local close-encounter claim. There were press reports, later books, sceptical reconstructions and declassified records. Few Las Palmas cases contain all those layers in one event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?id=38141" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa+2Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

It also helps readers separate three questions that are often blurred together. Did people see something unusual over the Canary Islands on 22 June 1976? Yes. Was the event important enough to enter Spain’s official UFO files? Yes. Does that mean a transparent craft with two giant occupants hovered near Gáldar? The evidence for that is weak and strongly disputed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es/BVMDefensa/exp_ovni/es/consulta/registro.do?id=38141" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es">[Biblioteca Virtual Defensa+2Naukas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliotecavirtual.defensa.gob.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gob.es.</span></span></span>

For the Las Palmas project, the most useful lesson is not that witnesses are foolish or that official files are meaningless. It is that witness testimony is powerful but fragile. A professional witness can describe a genuine event in good faith while still misjudging its distance, size and nature. An official file can preserve a mystery as it appeared to investigators at the time, while later data can make a conventional explanation much more likely.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/43047669/Los_gigantes_de_G%C3%A1ldar_y_los_avistamientos_canarios" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF) Los gigantes de Gáldar y los avistamientos canarios</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PDF) Los gigantes de Gáldar y los avistamientos canarios</span></span></span>

The Gáldar case therefore belongs at the centre of Las Palmas UFO history, but not as proof of alien visitation. It belongs there because it shows the full life cycle of a major UFO story: an extraordinary visual event, an unforgettable witness account, official attention, media amplification, and a later explanation that shifted the strongest evidence away from a close encounter and towards a Cold War missile plume over the Atlantic.
