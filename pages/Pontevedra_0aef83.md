## Why Pontevedra’s UFO record looks different

Spain’s official UFO archive matters because it gives readers a baseline: not every famous local story became a military file. The Ministry of Defence’s published list contains 83 entries, including classic Spanish aviation and radar cases, and the Galician cases highlighted in contemporary reporting are Ferrol in 1966, Becerreá-Lugo in 1969, and two Noia cases in 1989 and 1993. Those places are in A Coruña and Lugo, not Pontevedra. ## The Minguela case: Pontevedra’s most famous local UFO story

The best-known Pontevedra-linked case centres on Juan Bautista Minguela, a popular Vigo hairdresser who said he saw a large green luminous object near his house at Viladesuso, in the municipality of Oia, on the coast south of Vigo. Later accounts place the incident on 27 March 1974, with the story appearing in local press the following day and being revisited many times in Vigo and Galician media. ## Vigo and the Ría as a local “hotspot” in memory

Many Pontevedra UFO stories cluster around Vigo, the Ría de Vigo, Oia, Cangas, Moaña and the Atlantic-facing coast. That does not prove the area is objectively a UFO hotspot. It may instead reflect population density, a strong local press tradition, dramatic coastal visibility, busy maritime and air traffic, and the cultural magnetism of the Cíes Islands and the open Atlantic.

A 2023 regional feature on Vigo UFO stories placed Minguela alongside other local claims, including a reported 1970 sighting over Vigo associated in later accounts with the Jesuit Pedro Pablo Requejo and a film allegedly taken by Matías Álvarez García, then linked to Banco Pastor. Later summaries say the object was visible over Vigo for a period in the early evening and was interpreted by UFO writers as a large, high-altitude object. ## Likely explanations and recurring doubts

The most common weakness in Pontevedra’s best-known UFO material is not that witnesses must have invented everything. It is that the records often lack the independent checks needed to narrow the explanation. A useful assessment asks what would be needed to move each case from “interesting story” to “strong anomaly”.

For the Minguela case, the crucial missing pieces are a contemporaneous technical examination of the alleged ground trace, photographs with reliable chain of custody, independent witnesses who saw the same object at the same time, and environmental analysis of the rock and oil residue. The real Polycommander spill gives the story a concrete physical backdrop, but it also complicates it: oil-stained rocks, weathering, sea action, cleaning, heat, light and memory all become possible factors before invoking anything extraordinary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Juan-Garrido-6/publication/315475250_Que_aprendimos_de_la_catastrofe_del_Prestige_What_we_learnt_from_the_Prestige%27s_oil-spill_In_Spanish_and_abstracts_in_English_in_the_last_pages_of_the_book/links/58d14024aca272380eca233e/Que-aprendimos-de-la-catastrofe-del-Prestige-What-we-learnt-from-the-Prestiges-oil-spill-In-Spanish-and-abstracts-in-English-in-the-last-pages-of-the-book.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate+2Cadena SER]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate¿Qué aprendimos de la catástrofe del Prestige?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate¿Qué aprendimos de la catástrofe del Prestige?</span></span></span>

## What Pontevedra adds to Spanish UFO history

Pontevedra’s value is not that it supplies Spain’s strongest official UFO file. It adds something subtler: a coastal, media-rich, witness-led chapter in which UFO claims became part of local identity. The Ría de Vigo provides the geography, the 1970s provide the cultural mood, the press provides the amplifier, and later television and internet retellings keep the stories circulating.

The most responsible reading is therefore neither dismissal nor belief. Something was reported near Viladesuso in 1974, and something unusual was claimed by an aircrew off Vigo in 1997. Both stories deserve a place in a province-level UFO history. But the evidence available in public sources supports careful classification, not certainty: Pontevedra is a province of memorable UFO narratives, not a province where the best-known UFO claims have been publicly resolved by strong official documentation.
